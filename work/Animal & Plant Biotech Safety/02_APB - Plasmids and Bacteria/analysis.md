This presentation provides an overview of the process of transformation. It introduces plasmids and how they are utilized in genetic transformation. Transformation is one method of modern genetic modification. A transgenic organism has a genetic makeup containing DNA which was physically transferred from another organism. Examples of transformed organisms include oil eating microbes, pest resistant crops, and pharmaceuticals from biopharming practices. Plasmids are extra chromosomal DNA arranged in a circular shape. Plasmid DNA does not interfere with the host chromosome functions. Restriction enzymes are used to cut plasmids. A single cut will open a plasmid molecule, making way for the insertion of new genetic material. Once a plasmid molecule is closed, it will replicate within the host. A bacteria takes up small pieces of DNA. The plasmid DNA may be expressed. During the next few activities, you will attempt to transform bacteria with the p-glo plasmid. The p-glo plasmid has two major functions, bioluminescence and ampicillin resistance. Bioluminescence, or the glowing characteristic, is a result of the addition of the green fluorescent protein, or GFP. In order to activate the GFP, a rabinose is required. Bioluminescence indicates whether transformation has taken place. The E. coli strain HB101 is a benign bacterial strain, not to be confused with the more dangerous strains. E. coli HB101 is widely used in research. Non-transformed E. coli HB101 will not survive in the presence of ampicillin, a common antibacterial agent. In the media you prepared, you have various combinations of auger, ampicillin, p-glo plasmids, and a rabinose.
