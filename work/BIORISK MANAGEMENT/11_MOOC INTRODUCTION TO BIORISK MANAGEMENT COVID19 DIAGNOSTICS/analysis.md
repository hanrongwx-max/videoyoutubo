I am your instructor, Kenneth, and in this segment, I will be discussing the processing of samples from COVID-19 patients. COVID-19 patient samples can be in the form of mesopharyngeal swabs in viral transport medium or in the form of serum samples, blood samples, fecal samples, or urine samples. These will arrive at your facility with the appropriate media required for storage. Upon arrival at your facility, the first step involves cataloging of the samples. This can be done in collaboration with the hospital or the laboratory which has provided the samples. Cataloging of the samples is generally done by coding each sample as patient data must be protected. The next step involves opening of the samples in a biological safety cabinet. All samples derived from patients must be opened in the safety of a biological safety cabinet in accordance with standard operating procedures. Once you have opened these samples, you need to go to the next step which is inactivation of these samples. Inactivation is achieved by heating the sample to a specific temperature or a specific duration of time. In our case, we will heat the sample to 70 degrees Celsius for a duration of 60 minutes. After this procedure, the next step involves extraction of the nucleic acids. COVID-19 is an RNA virus, so we use an RNA extraction kit to extract the viral nucleic acids. Viral nucleic acids are extremely unstable and must be stored on ice immediately after extraction. It is advisable to process the sample directly after receiving it from the patient and proceed to RNA extraction. Once the RNA is extracted, the next step involves quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction. The sample is processed using a real-time thermal cycler. Now, as the virus is an RNA virus, it must be converted to cDNA or complementary DNA by the action of a reverse transcriptase. Once the sample of the RNA is reverse transcribed into cDNA, the next step will involve application of specific primers, which will amplify regions of the genome, which are of importance or relevance to the technicians and the clinicians. So, we use three basic regions of the genome, the RDRP, the E-gene and the S-gene. These are all genes associated with the viral particles. Now, as the virus is being translated and transcribed in the human cell, these copies of RNA may be present in different amounts. So, in a particular RNA sample, you may have variable number of copies of that particular viral DNA or the complementary DNA of the RNA virus. This needs to be taken into account when one does the analysis, as the copy numbers of different genes may have variability. So, that is a basic overview of the process of handling COVID-19 patient samples. To summarize, it begins with the deactivation of the sample by heat, followed by the extraction of the viral nucleic acid, which is the RNA using an RNA extraction procedure. And finally, the sample is processed by quantitative real-time PCR, in which case we reverse transcribe the viral nucleic acid into cDNA and the cDNA is amplified using locus specific primers for the viral proteins. With that, I would like to end this segment. If you have any questions, you may post them at the end of this video. Thank you for participating in this MOOC. Stay bio-safe. |
