The University of Chicago prohibits hazing. A person commits hazing when he or she knowingly requires a student or other person at the university to perform any act, on or off university property, for the purpose of induction, admission, or membership into any group, team, organization, or society that is connected to the university. This act is not sanctioned or authorized by the university and results in harm to any person or could reasonably be foreseen to result in such harm. A person's willingness to participate in an act of hazing does not justify or excuse the act or fall outside of this policy. Some examples of hazing include the creation of physical or mental exhaustion or fatigue, course consumption of alcohol or other intoxicants, physical and mental shocks, paddling, physically or psychologically dangerous or risky activities, coercive, degrading, or humiliating games and activities, appropriation of public or private property, physical harm, and pressuring or coercing any person to participate in activities that are inconsistent with the university's community standards, policies, or applicable law. This list is intended to be illustrative and not all inclusive. Any student or group that commits hazing will be subject to discipline under college housing discipline or the germane student disciplinary system is appropriate. A staff member, post-doctoral researcher, academic employee, or anyone with a visiting appointment who commits hazing will be subject to discipline under the applicable disciplinary processes. In addition, hazing is a crime under Illinois law. The state's attorney may bring criminal charges against any person responsible for hazing. Any person with knowledge of hazing is expected to communicate promptly with the Office of the Vice President of Campus Life, the Office of the Provost, the University of Chicago Police Department, or University Human Resources. Reports may also be made anonymously using the university's whistleblower hotline, the number provided below. Next, we will discuss protest and demonstration on campus. The primary function of a university is to discover and disseminate knowledge by means of research and teaching. To fulfill this function, a free interchange of ideas is necessary not only within the university, but also with the larger society. At the University of Chicago, freedom of expression is vital to our shared goal of the pursuit of knowledge. Such freedom comes with the responsibility to welcome and promote this freedom for all. The right of freedom of expression at the university includes peaceful protests and orderly demonstrations. At the same time, the university has long recognized that the right to protest and demonstrate does not include the right to engage in conduct that disrupts the university's operations or endangers the safety of others. Conduct of members of the university disruptive of the operations of the university, including interference with instruction, research, administrative operations, freedom of association, and meetings is prohibited in subject to disciplinary action. Please see Building Occupancy Limits and Access linked in the dig deeper section of this module. Adherence to building closures is expected of people participating in a protestor demonstration within a university building, and university officials will require that protesters or demonstrators leave at the time of building closure.
